The Problem

Why most solar projects
in Africa stall or fail.

Africa's solar sector is growing fast. But money, time, and projects are being lost every year to the same three avoidable mistakes.

Problem 01

No local specialist
solar engineering expertise.

Designing a solar mini-grid is highly technical work — load forecasting, system sizing, protection coordination, single-line diagrams, HOMER Pro and PVSyst modelling. Most engineering firms in Africa are generalists who lack this depth. The result is under-designed systems that underperform, overdesigned ones that waste capital, or proposals that simply don't work on site.

Problem 02

Engineering costs that
derail project economics.

International engineering firms charge fees that make small and mid-size solar projects unviable. So developers cut corners — using unqualified engineers or skipping proper feasibility studies altogether. The short-term saving becomes a long-term cost: wrong equipment sizing, inflated bills of materials, and construction budgets that bear no relation to what the project actually needs.

Problem 03

No one accountable
once construction starts.

Most consultants hand over a design and walk away. From there the developer is alone — chasing NERC, REA and FMEnv approvals, managing the EPC contractor, and verifying that what gets built matches what was designed. Without an independent party managing delivery, projects drift: equipment is substituted, milestones slip, defects go unrecorded, and the asset that finally switches on is not the one that was financed.

Don't let any of these problems kill your project.

There is a better way.
