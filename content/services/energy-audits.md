Most commercial and industrial sites pay for power three ways: grid tariffs, diesel and lost production when supply fails. An energy audit puts numbers on all three. It shows which savings cost nothing, which pay back within months, and what is left for solar and storage to cover. Cut the waste first and the solar system you buy is smaller and cheaper.

## Who it is for

- **Manufacturers** running motors, compressors and process loads on grid and diesel
- **Hotels and estates** with large cooling, pumping and night-time loads
- **Telecom and infrastructure operators** managing many generator-backed sites
- **Any business** about to buy solar, that wants the system sized on real demand rather than a supplier's estimate

## What the audit covers

- **Measured load data.** Logging at the main distribution board over normal operation, so the profile is measured, not guessed.
- **Generators.** Run-hours, fuel use per kilowatt-hour at the loads you actually run, and how far below their efficient range the sets are operating.
- **Grid supply.** Hours of supply, tariff band and the real cost per kilowatt-hour once outages are counted.
- **Major loads.** Motors, compressors, chillers and air conditioning, pumps and lighting, and how they are controlled.
- **Power quality.** Power factor and anything else that is costing you money or capacity.

## What you get

A report with every measure ranked by payback:

- **No-cost measures**, such as scheduling, generator loading and controls settings
- **Low-cost measures**, such as power factor correction, leak repairs and lighting
- **Capital measures**, such as variable speed drives and replacing inefficient equipment
- **The solar and storage opportunity**, sized on the demand that remains after the savings, with the fuel and grid cost it would displace

Each measure comes with its estimated saving, cost and payback, so you can decide what to fund and in what order.

## Why audit before you buy solar

A solar quote is only as good as the load data behind it. If a supplier sizes your system on last year's bills, they size it for the waste as well. An audit separates what you need from what you are losing. It also gives you an independent specification you can put out to tender, so quotes can be compared like for like.
