Most development finance institutions and many commercial lenders will not fund a solar project without an environmental and social assessment they can rely on. A weak ESIA delays financial close. A strong one answers the reviewer's questions before they are asked. Heliora prepares ESIAs for solar and mini-grid projects to the IFC Performance Standards and to the environmental law of the country concerned.

Who it is for

  • Developers seeking DFI, impact or commercial debt that applies IFC Performance Standards
  • Electrification programmes that require environmental and social safeguards for every site
  • EPC contractors and sponsors who need an environmental and social management plan for construction

What we deliver

  • Screening and scoping. Whether the project needs a full ESIA or a lighter assessment, based on its scale, location and the funder's requirements.
  • Baseline studies. The environmental and social conditions on and around the site before construction.
  • Stakeholder engagement. Consultation with communities, landowners and authorities, documented the way reviewers expect.
  • Impact assessment and mitigation. Each impact rated, with a practical measure to avoid, reduce or manage it.
  • Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP). Who does what, when, and how it is monitored, during construction and operation.
  • Gender and inclusion assessment. How the project affects women and vulnerable groups, and how benefits can reach them.
  • Permit support. In Nigeria, preparation for the Federal Ministry of Environment process under the EIA Act. Elsewhere, support with the national environmental authority's requirements.

Issues specific to solar and mini-grids

  • Land and livelihoods. Ground-mounted arrays take land that may be farmed or used informally, even when the title looks clear.
  • Batteries. Safe storage, fire risk, and a credible plan for end-of-life collection and recycling.
  • Construction health and safety. Electrical work, working at height and traffic on rural roads.
  • Community health and safety. Distribution networks, connections and power houses in populated areas.
  • Labour and supply chain. Working conditions on site, and the supply-chain questions lenders increasingly raise about solar equipment.

Common questions

Do we need a full ESIA? Not always. Smaller projects may only need screening and a management plan. It depends on the project's scale and location and on the funder's categorisation. Screening tells you which applies before you spend on a full study.

How long does it take? Typically six to ten weeks, depending on the baseline work and stakeholder engagement required. The proposal fixes the timeline and milestones.

Will our lender accept it? We structure every ESIA to the IFC Performance Standards and to the review criteria funders publish, and we support you through the lender's review. Acceptance always remains the lender's decision.
